Supplement Evidence Database

Sources: Cochrane Reviews, PubMed, peer-reviewed journals
Total entries: 189 supplement-condition pairs

Evidence Scale:
0 = HARMFUL — evidence of harm, avoid
1 = No meaningful evidence
2 = Slight/preliminary evidence
3 = Moderate evidence, more research needed
4 = Good evidence from quality trials
5 = Strong evidence from multiple trials
6 = Very strong evidence, well-established


HARMFUL SUPPLEMENTS (Score 0) — AVOID

Aloe vera for digestion — HARMFUL. Ingestion causes diarrhea, electrolyte imbalance and kidney dysfunction. Source: Journal of Environmental Science and Health.

Antioxidants for mortality — Taking antioxidants have no health benefits. Beta carotene, vitamin A and vitamin E appear to increase risk of death. Source: Cochrane.

Dong quai for menopause in breast cancer patients — HARMFUL. Long term safety data is non-existent. May increase risk of cancer. Source: Lau et al. 2005.

Folic acid + vitamin B12 for cancer — POTENTIALLY HARMFUL. When combined with vitamin B12 can be associated with increased cancer risk. Source: Cochrane.

Fish oil / omega 3 for prostate cancer — HARMFUL? Recent studies have linked fish oil intake with increased prostate cancer risk. Source: Journal of the National Cancer Institute.

Niacin (vitamin B3) for heart disease — POTENTIALLY HARMFUL. Has long been thought effective for heart disease, but recent findings show it does not help and can have severe adverse effects. Source: New England Journal of Medicine.

Pau d'arco as decongestant — HARMFUL. Highly toxic. If used at all, should be used with extreme caution. Source: Oncology Nursing Forum.

Valerian for insomnia — HARMFUL? No significant differences between valerian and placebo. May have adverse side effects. Source: Sleep Medicine Reviews.

Vitamin A (high dose) for birth defects — HARMFUL. High doses (10,000 IU/day) can cause birth defects. Source: PubMed.

Vitamin E (high dose) for mortality — HARMFUL? High dosages might increase all-cause mortality. Source: American College of Physicians.


STRONG EVIDENCE (Score 5-6)

Coffee for heart disease — Score 6. A metastudy found the more coffee a person drinks, the lower their risk of cardiovascular disease. Optimum: 3-5 cups per day. Source: Epidemiology and Prevention.

Agnus castus (vitex) for PMS — Score 5. Helps improve PMS symptoms. Source: Archives of Gynecology and Obstetrics.

Caffeine for memory — Score 5. Enhances consolidation of long-term memories. Improves memory in young adults during non-optimal time of day. Source: Borota et al 2014; Frontiers in Psychology.

Calcium + vitamin D for bone health — Score 5. Review of 8 studies (30,970 participants): reduced total fracture risk by 15% and hip fracture risk by 30%. Source: Osteoporosis International.

Cannabis for Multiple Sclerosis — Score 5. Improved muscle spasms, pain, sleep quality, and mobility. Cannabis-based drug Sativex approved in 30 countries. Source: Journal of Ethnopharmacology.

Eggshell membrane for joints/arthritis — Score 5. Increases cell production and decreases inflammation. 500mg/day gives rapid results. Source: Cochrane.

Feverfew for migraines — Score 5. Reduces migraine frequency by just over half a migraine per month. Source: Cochrane.

Fish oil / omega 3 in pregnancy for preterm birth — Score 5. Meta-analysis concluded preterm and early preterm birth were reduced in women receiving omega-3. Source: Cochrane.

Folic acid for birth defects — Score 5. Helps prevent birth defects, but timing of supplementation is important. Source: Cochrane.

Hawthorn for chronic heart failure — Score 5. Improves heart function, increases functional capacity, alleviates symptoms, improves quality of life. Very favourable safety profile. Source: American Journal of Cardiovascular Drugs.

Peppermint oil for IBS — Score 5. Relaxes muscles, relieves IBS-related spasms. May be more effective than conventional anti-spasmodics. Source: Cochrane.

Probiotics for antibiotic-associated diarrhea — Score 5. Effective for preventing diarrhea associated with antibiotic use. Source: Cochrane.

Zinc for pneumonia in children — Score 5. Reduces incidence and prevalence of pneumonia in children. Source: Cochrane, WHO.
