Supplement Evidence Database

A comprehensive evidence-based reference guide containing ratings for 189 supplements across
multiple health conditions, sourced from Cochrane reviews, PubMed, and peer-reviewed journals.

Evidence Scale:
0 = Harmful or potentially harmful (avoid)
1 = No evidence or insufficient evidence
2 = Slight evidence (preliminary or low-quality studies)
3 = Moderate evidence (some supporting research, more needed)
4 = Good evidence (well-supported by quality research)
5 = Strong evidence (robust clinical trial support)
6 = Very strong evidence (extensive, high-quality research)

SECTION 1: HARMFUL SUPPLEMENTS (SCORE 0) - CRITICAL WARNINGS
==========================================================================

WARNING: The following supplements have been rated as harmful or potentially harmful
based on clinical evidence. They should be avoided or used only under strict medical
supervision.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Aloe Vera (Ingestion) - HARMFUL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Evidence Score: 0
Type: Plant / Herb
Category: Digestion (Digestive Aid)
Notes: HARMFUL. Never eat. Ingestion can cause diarrhea, electrolyte imbalance and kidney dysfunction.
Source: Journal of Environmental Science and Health
Research: http://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/10590500600614303#.VcDwRZNVhBc

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Evidence Score: 0
Type: Compound

Calcium - Blood Pressure
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Evidence Score: 4 (Good Evidence)
Type: Mineral
Notes: High quality evidence has shown that extra calcium can slightly reduce blood pressure, especially in people younger than 35.
Source: Cochrane
Research: http://www.cochrane.org/CD010037/HTN_extra-calcium-to-prevent-high-blood-pressure

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Chlorella - Blood Pressure, Cholesterol, Cardiovascular Disease
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Evidence Score: 4 (Good Evidence)
Alternative Name: Algae
Type: Alga
Notes: Significantly decreases blood pressure, blood glucose, and some cholesterols. Meta-analysis on 19 RCTs with 797 subjects indicated that Chlorella administration significantly decreased the levels of total cholesterol, low-density lipoprotein cholesterol, systolic blood pressure, diastolic blood pressure, and fasting blood glucose whereas changes in triglycerides, high-density lipoprotein cholesterol, and body mass index were not statistically significant.
Source: Clinical Nutrition
Research: https://www.clinicalnutritionjournal.com/article/S0261-5614(17)31351-1/fulltext

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Chocolate, Dark - Blood Pressure
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Evidence Score: 3 (Moderate Evidence)
Type: Plant / Herb
Notes: Chemicals known as flavanols, found in cocoa products, are thought to widen blood vessels and cause a minor drop in blood pressure. Further trials are needed to ascertain long-term efficacy. Does not apply to milk chocolate.
Source: Cochrane
Research: http://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD008893.pub2/abstract

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Coconut Oil - Obesity
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Evidence Score: 3 (Moderate Evidence)
Type: Plant / Herb
Category: Cardio / General Health
Notes: 30ml of coconut oil per day might help to reduce waist size, but possibly because the fat in coconut oil is not as easily stored.
Source: Cochrane
Research: http://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/o/cochrane/clcentral/articles/529/CN-00721529/frame.html

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Coffee - Heart Disease
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Evidence Score: 6 (Very Strong Evidence)
Type: Plant / Herb
Notes: A metastudy found that the more coffee a person drinks, the lower their risk of cardiovascular disease. The optimum amount of coffee for this benefit was 3-5 per day.
Source: Epidemiology and Prevention
Research: http://circ.ahajournals.org/content/129/6/643.long

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Evidence Score: 1 (No Evidence)
Alternative Name: Ubiquinol
Type: Enzyme
Notes: Pooled data from two trials showed that CoQ10 did not affect blood pressure compared to placebo.
Source: Cochrane
Research: http://www.cochrane.org/CD007435/HTN_coenzyme-q10-high-blood-pressure

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Ecklonia Cava Seaweed - Cholesterol, Weight Loss
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Evidence Score: 3 (Moderate Evidence)
Type: Alga
Category: Weight Control / Cardio
Notes: There is evidence that seaweed supplements can combat several mechanisms behind the onset of cardiovascular disease. Ecklonia in particular has been shown to decrease cholesterol. A review suggests there is evidence that diet supplementation with whole macroalgae or products of macroalgae origin can ameliorate several mechanisms underlying the onset and propagation of CVDs. One study of ecklonia cava in particular (80 subjects) concluded that ECE supplementation improved blood lipid profiles through decreasing total cholesterol and LDL-cholesterol levels which are known as major cardiovascular risk factors. More studies are needed to establish the long-term safety and effectiveness.
Source: Marine Drugs
Research: https://www.mdpi.com/1660-3397/13/11/6838/htm

--------------------------------------------------------------------------

Evidence Score: 1 (No Evidence)
Type: Plant / Herb

Hawthorn - Chronic Heart Failure
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Evidence Score: 5 (Strong Evidence)
Alternative Name: Crataegus spp.
Type: Fruit
Notes: Hawthorn improves heart function in those with chronic heart failure. Randomized, controlled trials in patients with heart failure have demonstrated that hawthorn increases functional capacity, alleviates disabling symptoms, and improves health-related quality of life. It has a very favourable safety profile both as monotherapy and add-on therapy.
Source: American Journal of Cardiovascular Drugs
Research: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40256-017-0249-9
